## 1. What I learned

During Week 1, I focused on building a practical baseline across AI concepts, solution selection, Git/GitHub, business analysis for AI, and evidence-based consulting.

### AI and solution foundations

I can explain and distinguish the basic roles of:

- Artificial Intelligence
- Machine Learning
- Deep Learning
- Generative AI
- Large Language Models
- workflow automation
- AI agents
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation as a solution pattern

The main principle I applied throughout the week was:

> Use the simplest solution that adequately solves the business problem.

This means that solution selection should start from the problem, required outcome, available data and constraints rather than from a preferred technology.

Examples of the reasoning practised during the week:

- fixed and predictable process → workflow or rules-based automation;
- historical labelled examples + known target outcome → potentially supervised machine learning;
- discovering patterns without predefined labels → potentially unsupervised machine learning;
- summarisation, drafting or other generative language tasks → potentially Generative AI / LLM;
- answering questions based on a controlled document set → potentially retrieval + LLM;
- dynamic multi-step work requiring tool selection and adaptation → potentially an AI agent.

These are candidate solution patterns rather than automatic technology choices. Business value, data readiness, risk, cost and operational constraints still need to be assessed.

## 2. Business Analysis for AI

I applied Business Analysis techniques to several AI-related scenarios and use cases.

For each case, I practised identifying:

- business problem;
- users and stakeholders;
- desired outcome;
- business KPIs;
- current process;
- candidate solution type;
- required data and inputs;
- human responsibilities;
- risks and constraints;
- privacy and security concerns;
- assumptions;
- success criteria;
- acceptance and evaluation criteria.

A key learning point was that an AI use case requires more than functional requirements.

Depending on the problem, the analysis may also require:

- data requirements;
- non-functional requirements;
- privacy and security requirements;
- governance requirements;
- controls;
- assumptions;
- constraints;
- risks;
- project prerequisites;
- organisational change activities.

I also started applying the principle that important AI requirements should be linked to measurable acceptance criteria, evaluation criteria or other observable evidence.
